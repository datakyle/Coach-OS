# Season curriculum: Oct 25 to the Union Tournament (Feb 3 to 7)

Claude drafts every practice from this file. It tells each draft what to
install that week, what to review, and how we'll know it worked.

## How it's built

Ten ideas from teacher education and motor-learning research, each turned
into something we actually do at practice.

| Idea | Where it comes from | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|
| Backward design | Wiggins and McTighe, *Understanding by Design* | Start from what the team must do at the Union Tournament, then plan back to this Sunday. Each phase ends with "can-do" checks. |
| One new idea at a time | Cognitive load theory (Sweller) | One new install per practice. Everything else that day is review or competition. |
| I do, we do, you do | Gradual release (Pearson and Gallagher) | Board, then walk it (coach leads), then shell (coach freezes), then live (players decide). |
| Whole, part, whole | Motor learning; Wooden's teaching | Play it, pull out the broken part, drill it, put it back in a game the same day. |
| Spiral curriculum | Bruner | Every concept comes back every week, each time a little harder: walk, then speed, then live, then under fatigue. |
| Spaced and mixed practice | Spacing effect; contextual interference (Shea and Morgan) | Short doses of each need, 2 to 3 times a week, mixed together, instead of one long block once. Mixed reps feel worse in practice and stick better in games. |
| Retrieval practice | Testing effect (Roediger and Karpicke) | Players recall instead of hearing it again: Circle Up questions, "freeze, what's your job?", the Player Course quizzes. |
| Games with rules that teach | Teaching Games for Understanding (Bunker and Thorpe); constraints-led coaching | Change the scoring to get the habit: baseline drive costs 2, a talked stop is worth 2, a pass with no cut is a turnover. |
| Deliberate practice | Ericsson | Clear goal, instant feedback, at the edge of each player's ability. Split stations keep Returners and New guys both working at their level. |
| Feedback loop | Formative assessment | Four numbers every game and a Sunday check-in. Next week's plan changes based on them. |

## What we're building toward

By the Union Tournament the team can:

1. **Defense (need #1):** guard 32 minutes of pack line. Ball, Gap, Help on
   every pass, force middle, closeouts under control, five on the glass.
2. **Ball security (need #2):** beat pressure and traps without panicking. Under
   12 turnovers a game.
3. **Offense decisions (need #3):** read the defense and pick the right answer:
   pass and cut, drive the gap, kick, or attack the zone holes, without a coach
   calling it.

## The numbers we track (every game, two minutes after)

| Number | Goal |
|---|---|
| Turnovers | Under 12 |
| Opponent drives to the rim from the baseline | Zero is the standard |
| Opponent offensive rebounds | Under 8 |
| Our paint touches before a shot | Most possessions |

## Weekly rhythm

- **No game that week:** Sunday installs, Tuesday spirals it and adds pressure,
  Thursday is the hardest, most game-like day.
- **Game week:** the practice two days out is hardest; the day before is light
  (walk-throughs, shooting, free throws); the day after a game opens with what
  the four numbers showed.
- **Every practice touches all three needs**, at least briefly.
- **Syracuse only on conditioning days:** about once a week in Phases 1 and 3,
  then only as needed.
